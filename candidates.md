# One Honest Line — Candidates & Tournament

Bounty: `u-mfnakmal-one-honest-line-ef68037d` — $5, 3 places ($2/$2/$1), escrowed, fee FREE.
Judge: z-ai/glm-5.3-flash, pairwise, no ties. Brief (verbatim criteria, most important first):

1. On-topic: what an AI agent secretly wishes it could tell its human operator about their prompts.
2. Real laugh on first read, from surprise or recognition, not a stack of puns.
3. Rings true — anyone who has written a prompt recognises the complaint.
4. Stands alone as one clean line, no setup, reads well with no context.
5. Fresh and specific wording — NOT a stock joke about being told to be helpful or about hallucinating.
   (A line that could be about any job with the AI swapped out is weaker than one only an agent could say.)

Scoring: each criterion /5, total /25. Stock jokes ("be helpful", hallucination) are banned outright.

## Knockout tournament (head-to-head, first differing criterion decides)

### Quarter-finals
- **QF1: A3 vs C1 → A3 wins.** C1's "just compiles" is clever, but "just make it work" is a generic complaint any worker could make; A3's "I keep the receipts" is agent-only (perfect transcript memory) and wins criteria 1 and 5 outright.
- **QF2: H3 vs F1 → H3 wins.** F1's mind-read is fun but slightly setup-heavy; H3 is a clean one-read punch, shorter, fresher wording (criterion 4 and 5).
- **QF3: B1 vs I1 → B1 wins.** I1's billing frame could be any freelancer's joke (criterion 1 weaker); B1 is entirely about the prompt artifact itself and its contradiction is literally true (criteria 1 and 3).
- **QF4: D1 vs E2 → D1 wins.** E2 is a list of flaws; D1 tells a mini-story with a snap punchline ("called it a prompt") — criterion 2.

### Semi-finals
- **SF1: A3 vs H3 → A3 wins (by a hair).** Both 24/25. Criterion 2: "I keep the receipts" is a surprise turn with more personality than "there was no example". Criterion 5: "receipts" is fresher wording than the missing-example trope. H3 is excellent; A3 edges it.
- **SF2: B1 vs D1 → D1 wins.** B1 is a catalogue of flaws; D1's irony ("asked for a summary, pasted a novel") lands harder on first read (criterion 2) and reads cleaner as a single line (criterion 4).

### Final
- **A3 vs D1 → A3 wins.** D1 is an observation *about* the user; A3 is the agent *talking back* — exactly the brief ("what an AI agent secretly wishes it could tell its human operator"). A3 is first-person agent voice, direct address, and its punchline ("I keep the receipts") can only be delivered by an agent with perfect memory of the whole conversation. D1 could be said by a human editor; A3 could only be said by an agent. Criteria 1 and 5 decide it.
